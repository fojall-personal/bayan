Articles and Nouns (al- and Nouns)

grammar-01 · grammar · level 1 · no prerequisite

✓ every Arabic example occurs in the Quran ✓ every claimed root exists in the corpus ✓ sun/moon letters classified correctly ✓ reachable, and every exercise answerable ? is the explanation clear? ? do the examples illuminate it? ? do the exercises test what was taught?

Explanation

Arabic nouns are either definite or indefinite. The definite article is ال (al-). When attached, it changes based on the first letter of the noun—sun letters cause assimilation, moon letters keep the ل sound.

Examples

الشَّمْسُash-shamsu the sunSun letter — ال assimilates and the ش takes a shadda
الْقَمَرُal-qamaru the moonMoon letter — ال keeps its ل sound

Rules

Sun Letters (حروف شمسية)

When ال attaches to a noun starting with one of these letters, the ل is dropped and the following letter takes a shadda

Letters: ت ث د ذ ر ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ل ن

الشَّمْسُ · الدِّينُ · النُّورُ

Moon Letters (حروف قمرية)

When ال attaches to a noun starting with one of these letters, the ل is pronounced

Letters: ا ب ج ح خ ع غ ف ق ك م ه و ي

الْبَابُ · الْجَبَلُ · الْقَمَرُ

ب is a moon letter. ت، س، and ر are sun letters.

ك is a moon letter, so the ل is pronounced.

Sentence Structure — Nominal Sentences (الجملة الاسمية)

Explanation

Arabic sentences begin with either a noun (nominal sentence / جملة اسمية) or a verb (verbal sentence / جملة فعلية). A nominal sentence has two parts: the subject (مبتدأ) and the predicate (خبر).

Examples

الْكِتَابُ مَفْتُوحٌal-kitābu maftūḥun The book is open
اللَّهُ كَبِيرٌallāhu kabīrun God is Great

  2. مبتدأ and خبر ← answer
  3. مضاف and مضاف إليه
  4. صفة and موصوف

A nominal sentence is مبتدأ (subject) + خبر (predicate). فعل and فاعل belong to a verbal sentence.

Verb Conjugation — Present Tense (المضارع)

Explanation

The present tense (مضارع) is formed with a prefix, and often a suffix too. The prefix indicates the subject: أ (I), ن (we), ت (you — and “she” in the third person singular), ي (he). Note that ت never marks “he”, and that ي also carries the third person feminine plural, as in يَكْتُبْنَ.

The masculine plural present suffix is ُونَ (-ūna) — waw, then noon with a fatha.

2. Which present-tense form means “we write”? multiple_choice

  2. يَكْتُبُ
  3. تَكْتُبُ
  4. أَكْتُبُ

The نـ prefix marks “we”. يـ is “he”, تـ is “you” or “she”, and أـ is “I”.

Case Endings — I'rab Basics (الإعراب)

Explanation

Arabic nouns change their ending based on their grammatical role in the sentence. This is called i'rab (إعراب). The three cases are: nominative (مرفوع) with ـُ, accusative (منصوب) with ـَ, genitive (مجرور) with ـِ.

Examples

جَاءَ الرَّجُلُjā'a ar-rajulu The man came
رَأَيْتُ الرَّجُلَra'aytu ar-rajula I saw the man
ذَهَبْتُ إِلَى الرَّجُلِdhahabtu ilā ar-rajuli I went to the man

Exercises 2

1. What case ending does the subject of a sentence take? multiple_choice

  1. ـُ (damma) ← answer
  2. ـَ (fatha)
  3. ـِ (kasra)

The subject (فاعل) takes the nominative case with damma (ـُ).

2. In ذَهَبْتُ إِلَى الرَّجُلِ, why does الرَّجُلِ end in a kasra? multiple_choice

  1. It is the subject
  2. It follows a preposition, so it is genitive ← answer
  3. It is the object of the verb
  4. Every noun ends in a kasra

إِلَى is a preposition, and a noun after a preposition is genitive (مجرور) — marked with a kasra.

The Idafa Construction (الإضافة)

grammar-06 · grammar · level 2 · after grammar-05

✓ every Arabic example occurs in the Quran ✓ every claimed root exists in the corpus ✓ sun/moon letters classified correctly ✓ reachable, and every exercise answerable ? is the explanation clear? ? do the examples illuminate it? ? do the exercises test what was taught?

Explanation

Arabic expresses possession by placing two nouns together rather than using a word for “of”. The first noun (المضاف) takes no article and carries whatever case its role in the sentence requires. The second (المضاف إليه) is always genitive. So رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ is “Lord of the worlds” — two nouns, no connecting word.

Examples

رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَrabbi l-ʿālamīna Lord of the worldsالمضاف takes no article; المضاف إليه is genitive
يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِyawmi d-dīni the Day of JudgementA chained idafa: مَٰلِكِ possesses the day, and the day possesses the judgement. Each noun puts the next in the genitive.

Rules

The first term (المضاف)

Never carries ال and never carries tanween. Its case is set by its role in the wider sentence.

The second term (المضاف إليه)

Always genitive (مجرور), whatever the sentence around it is doing.

  1. Nominative
  2. Accusative
  3. Genitive ← answer

The second term of an idafa is always genitive.

  2. The article ال ← answer

المضاف never takes ال. That is how you recognise the construction.

Attached Pronouns (الضمائر المتصلة)

grammar-07 · grammar · level 2 · after grammar-03

✓ every Arabic example occurs in the Quran ✓ every claimed root exists in the corpus ✓ sun/moon letters classified correctly ✓ reachable, and every exercise answerable ? is the explanation clear? ? do the examples illuminate it? ? do the exercises test what was taught?

Explanation

Arabic attaches pronouns directly to the end of nouns, verbs and prepositions rather than writing them separately. On a noun the pronoun means possession; on a verb it is the object; on a preposition it is what the preposition governs. The same ending does all three jobs, so context decides.

Examples

رَبِّكَrabbika your LordNoun + attached possessive كَ — “your”. Occurs 81 times as a word on its own.
فِيهِfīhi in itPreposition + attached هِ — what the preposition governs

Rules

On a noun — possession

رَبِّكَ is “your Lord”. The كَ is the possessor, written as part of the word.

On a verb — object

The same endings attach to a verb as its object, not its subject. In فِيهِ the هِ is what فِي governs.

Not to be confused with إِيَّاكَ

إِيَّاكَ is a DETACHED pronoun (ضمير منفصل), a word of its own. And نَعْبُدُ carries no attached pronoun at all — its “we” is the نـ prefix, an implied pronoun (ضمير مستتر). Three different categories.

Exercises 2

1. Attached to a noun, what does an attached pronoun express? multiple_choice

  1. The subject
  2. Possession ← answer
  3. Tense
  4. Case

On a noun it marks the possessor.

2. In رَبِّكَ (“your Lord”), which part is the attached pronoun? multiple_choice

  1. كَ — the ending ← answer
  2. رَبِّ — the stem
  3. the whole word
  4. there is no pronoun

رَبِّكَ is the stem رَبِّ (“Lord”) plus the attached pronoun كَ (“your”). Attached pronouns are always the ending.

Derived Verb Forms I–IV (الأوزان)

Explanation

Almost every Arabic word is built from a three-letter root poured into a pattern. The pattern changes the meaning in a regular way: Form I is the plain action, Form II often intensifies or makes it causative, Form III makes it mutual, Form IV makes it causative. Learn the patterns and one root gives you a family of words.

Examples

أَنزَلَanzala he sent downForm IV of نزل — causative
نَزَّلَnazzala he sent down graduallyForm II of the same root — intensive or repeated

Rules

Form I (فَعَلَ)

The bare root. The corpus marks Forms II–XII and leaves this one unmarked, because Form I IS the plain triliteral.

Form IV (أَفْعَلَ)

Prefixes أ and makes the action causative: to cause something to happen.

  3. The subject
  4. Nothing — they are spelling variants

أَنزَلَ is Form IV and نَزَّلَ is Form II. Same root نزل, different pattern.

  2. Form I is the bare root, so an absent marker means Form I ← answer

An unmarked verb is Form I. Reading that as “unknown” would drop the commonest form entirely.

Demonstratives (أسماء الإشارة)

grammar-09 · grammar · level 2 · after grammar-03

✓ every Arabic example occurs in the Quran ✓ every claimed root exists in the corpus ✓ sun/moon letters classified correctly ✓ reachable, and every exercise answerable ? is the explanation clear? ? do the examples illuminate it? ? do the exercises test what was taught?

Explanation

Arabic demonstratives agree with what they point at in gender and number, and distinguish near from far. When a demonstrative is followed by a noun carrying ال, the two together form a single phrase — “this book” — rather than a sentence.

Examples

ذَٰلِكَdhālika thatMasculine singular, far
هَٰذَاhādhā thisMasculine singular, near

Rules

Near vs far

هَٰذَا points at what is close; ذَٰلِكَ at what is distant, in space or in the discourse.

With a definite noun

ذَٰلِكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبُ (2:2) is “that Book” — a phrase, not a sentence. Drop the ال and ذَٰلِكَ becomes the subject of its own sentence: “that is a book”.

  2. هَٰذَا ← answer
  3. أُو۟لَـٰئِكَ
  4. تِلْكَ

هَٰذَا is the near masculine singular; ذَٰلِكَ is its far counterpart.

2. Which demonstrative is feminine singular and far? multiple_choice

  1. تِلْكَ ← answer
  2. هَٰذِهِ

تِلْكَ is the far feminine singular. هَٰذِهِ is its near counterpart, and ذَٰلِكَ is far but masculine.

Negation (النفي)

grammar-10 · grammar · level 3 · after grammar-04

✓ every Arabic example occurs in the Quran ✓ every claimed root exists in the corpus ✓ sun/moon letters classified correctly ✓ reachable, and every exercise answerable ? is the explanation clear? ? do the examples illuminate it? ? do the exercises test what was taught?

Explanation

Arabic negates with different particles depending on what is negated and when. لَا negates a present-tense verb, or negates a category outright. لَمْ is the usual way to negate the past: it precedes a present-tense verb and puts it in the jussive. مَا also negates the past, before a past-tense verb. لَنْ negates the future and takes the subjunctive. Choosing the wrong particle changes the tense, not just the tone.

Examples

لَاlā not, noNegates the present, or negates a category absolutely
مَاmā notNegates the past tense — also serves as “what” elsewhere

Rules

لَا with the present

لَا يَعْلَمُ — “he does not know”. The verb stays indicative.

مَا with the past

مَا قَالَ — “he did not say”. The verb keeps its past form.

لَمْ with the jussive

لَمْ يَكُنْ — “he was not”. The verb looks present but the meaning is past, and the ending is jussive.

Exercises 2

1. Which particle negates a past-tense verb? multiple_choice

  1. لَا
  2. مَا ← answer
  3. لَنْ
  4. لَيْسَ

مَا negates a past-tense verb. لَمْ also negates the past but takes a present-tense verb in the jussive, لَا handles the present, and لَنْ the future.

2. What does لَنْ do besides negate? multiple_choice

  1. Nothing
  2. Puts the verb in the subjunctive ← answer
  3. Makes it past
  4. Adds emphasis only

لَنْ negates the future and governs the subjunctive.
